Most errors are between adjacent levels. The most frequent is $l_5$ predicted as $l_4$ (first for BERT, Llama~3 and Mistral, second for RoBERTa): $l_5$ is the single score 100, $l_4$ the range 75--99. Errors of two levels still affect 39 to 46 publishers per model, mostly between $l_2$ and $l_4$; errors of three or more levels affect 10 to 17.

Majority-vote ties are broken toward the \emph{lowest} level. This decides 13 of the 439 publishers for BERT, 20 for RoBERTa, 15 for Llama~3 and 11 for Mistral, always toward lower predicted reliability.

Figure~\ref{fig:cmPoliticalLeaning} shows the article-level confusion matrices for each leaning.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Slightly Right} (largest group, $N=7490$) and \textbf{Non labelled} are strongly diagonal; on Slightly Right accuracy is 66.4--69.0\%.

\end{itemize}
These matrices are descriptive: leaning and reliability are both publisher attributes and are associated in the corpus. Section~\ref{app:robustness} controls for this.


\section{Error Direction at the Article Level}
\label{app:error_direction}

Figure~\ref{fig:error_direction} gives the Mean Error Direction (MED) per true reliability level, computed over misclassified articles only. Errors regress toward the centre of the scale:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$l_1$ and $l_5$} can only be over- and underestimated respectively. The MED is about $+2.1$ for $l_1$ and about $-1.7$ for $l_5$ (BERT $-1.60$, RoBERTa $-1.72$, Llama $-1.67$, Mistral $-1.74$); most $l_5$ errors land on $l_4$.
  \item \textbf{$l_3$} has the smallest MED, $0.10$ to $0.35$: it absorbs overestimated $l_2$ and underestimated $l_4$ articles, and the two directions largely cancel.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Details}
\label{app:experimental_details}

\paragraph{Folds.}
All articles of a publisher (up to 200) stay in the same fold. Across folds, the share of each reliability class has a standard deviation of 1.37 to 3.61 percentage points.

\paragraph{Optimisation and batching.}
Tokenizers: \texttt{BertTokenizerFast}, \texttt{RobertaTokenizerFast}, and the causal \texttt{LlamaTokenizer} and \texttt{MistralTokenizer} with manual padding; Flash Attention~2 for Mistral. Batch sizes (training/evaluation): 32/16 for BERT, 16/8 for RoBERTa, 8/8 for Llama with gradient accumulation, 6/48 for Mistral with five accumulation steps. Only Llama~3 uses early stopping, on a stratified 10\% hold-out of the training articles (patience two epochs), restoring the state with the lowest validation loss; the other models run for a fixed number of epochs. No test fold informs training or model selection. A fixed seed (42) gives identical splits across models.


\section{Convergence Analysis}
\label{app:convergence}
In a preliminary 20-epoch run of Llama~3 (Figure~\ref{fig:loss_plot}), the validation loss is lowest at epoch 4 (0.4416) and the generalisation gap widens afterwards, so the other models are trained for five epochs. For Mistral (Figure~\ref{fig:loss_mistral}), the training loss averaged over folds falls from 2.023 to 0.355 and 0.231 in the first three epochs, so it is trained for three.
